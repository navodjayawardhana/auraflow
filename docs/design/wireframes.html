What this set is for

These plates fix structure before surface. Every decision recorded here — what the screen leads with, what gets a whole card, what is deliberately absent — was settled in greyscale, so it could be argued about on its merits rather than defended by how it looked.

The notes beside each frame are the substance. A wireframe that only shows boxes proves nothing; the reasoning in the margin is what makes it evidence of design rather than decoration.

How to read a plate

Each frame is drawn at the true target size, 390 × 844 pt, and reduced to fit. Numbered bubbles sit on the element they describe and match the numbered notes to the right.

Where a decision had a plausible alternative that was rejected, the alternative is recorded too — an unrecorded rejection is indistinguishable from an idea nobody had.

  1. The recovery ring is the hero

    One number answers the question people actually open a health app with — how am I today? Steps and water are real goals but they are progress, not judgement, so they become the two inner rings: present at a glance, subordinate by size. A grid of equal tiles would have made the app a data dump with no point of view.

    Rejected

    A four-tile summary grid with no ring. It scanned faster but left the user to synthesise the day themselves, which is the one job the app exists to do.

  2. Estimated figures carry a ≈ and say so

    Active energy is derived from step count, not measured. It gets the ≈ prefix and a caption naming the derivation. Measured values get provenance instead — "from last night". A value the phone cannot produce shows an em dash, never a zero, because zero is a reading and absence is not.

  3. The sheet overlaps the hero by 26 pt

    The overlap makes the lower half read as a surface lying on the hero rather than a second band beside it, which is what lets one screen hold a full-bleed graphic and a dense metric grid without feeling like two screens stapled together.

  4. Live hardware is the only inverted surface

    The ESP32 strip is the only place in the app showing data arriving in real time, so it is the only dark surface. The inversion is a status signal, not a style: when the node disconnects the strip is removed entirely rather than greyed, because an empty live readout invites the user to wonder whether the number is stale.

  5. Four tabs and a raised action

    See plate 09. The bar is inset from the screen edge so content scrolls beneath it, which signals there is more below without a scroll indicator.

  1. The hero collapses, it does not vanish

    At 100 pt the header keeps the screen name and the score. Scrolling past the ring must not cost the user the one number the ring existed to deliver — otherwise everything below it loses its reference point.

  2. The written brief sits above the charts

    Prose is the fastest way to deliver a relationship between two figures; a chart is the fastest way to deliver a trend. The brief explains why today differs from Tuesday, then the charts below let the user verify it. Reversing the order would make the user do the synthesis first and read the summary after they no longer need it.

  3. Generated text is stamped and bounded

    Timestamp and model on the card, a status pill, and a standing line that the text came from the user's own figures and is not medical advice. Three states are drawn for this card — see plate 10's note on states — because a brief that has not been written yet is a screen people will meet most mornings.

  4. Forecast bars are ranked, not absolute

    The model's hourly output occupies a narrow band. Plotted against an absolute 0–1 scale every bar is the same height and the chart says nothing, so height and weight are ranked against the day's own spread. The band behind the bars marks the recommended window.

  5. Model coverage is stated, not implied

    "17 of 25 inputs are your data" is on the face of the card, expandable for the rest. A forecast that hides how much of itself is assumption is the failure mode this whole design language exists to avoid.

  1. In and out, side by side and unequal

    Eaten is a counted figure; active energy is derived, so it keeps the ≈ it carries on Today. Showing them adjacent invites the comparison the user came for, and the proportional bar answers it without arithmetic.

  2. There is no net-calorie number

    Every competing app shows one. AuraFlow cannot: a net balance needs basal metabolic rate, which the app has no way to measure and will not silently assume. The card states the omission and its size — roughly 1,500 kcal — so the absence reads as a decision rather than a missing feature.

    Rejected

    Estimating BMR from a Mifflin–St Jeor formula and showing a net figure with a footnote. It would have been the single least defensible number in the app, and it would have undermined the ≈ convention everywhere else.

  3. Aggregates state their coverage

    Macros are summed over the three items that carry them, and the card says "from 3 of 4". A total that silently drops an item is worse than no total.

  4. Every row says where its number came from

    A barcode lookup and a typed guess are different kinds of number. Looked-up rows carry a barcode mark and the database name; user-entered rows carry "your estimate" and a ≈ on the value, with a dashed time badge. The distinction is visible in the list, not one tap away.

  1. Barcode first, because the camera is already there

    The AR movement feature brings camera permission with it, so a barcode scan costs the user nothing extra and returns a figure with a source. It gets the heaviest treatment on the screen; typing is the fallback, not the default path.

  2. Search results name their source and their basis

    "Open Food Facts · per 100 g" on every row. Per-100 g versus per-serving is the most common way a food log goes quietly wrong, so the basis is on the row rather than inside the detail view.

  3. The escape hatch is honest about what it is

    Not every meal is in a database — a rice and curry plate rarely is. Manual entry stays available but is labelled at the point of entry as an estimate, and the field block is drawn dashed to match the badge those entries will carry in the list.

    Rejected

    Hiding manual entry behind a "can't find it?" link. It made the honest path feel like a failure state, when it is how a large share of Sri Lankan meals will actually be logged.

  4. Offline is stated at the commit point

    The app is offline-first, so the button says what happens if the network is down instead of failing after the tap. The user finds out before they act, not after.

  1. Scope is declared before the first message

    A standing note at the top of the thread says what the assistant can see and what it is not. Placing it in the conversation rather than a settings screen means it is read by everyone, once, at the moment it is relevant.

  2. Replies cite the figures they used

    Chips under each answer name the readings it drew on — "sleep · Tue", "baseline · 14 d". The user can check an answer against the dashboard instead of taking it on trust, which is the difference between an assistant grounded in their data and one that sounds like it is.

  3. Suggested questions, not an empty box

    An empty prompt field gives no indication of what the assistant can usefully answer. Two suggestions set the scope by example and give a one-tap path for the majority case.

  4. Connection state lives in the header

    This is the one screen that stops working offline — the dashboard does not. The status line, the placeholder text and the send control all change together, and an amber banner explains that the rest of the app is unaffected.

  1. Today is the emphasised bar, not a separate readout

    The current day is drawn solid against six lighter bars, so "where am I relative to my week" is answered by position rather than by a second number the user has to hold in their head.

  2. Provisional days are marked in the series

    A score computed before the 14-day baseline exists is a different kind of reading, so it is drawn differently — dashed here, violet in the hi-fi set. Never greyed and never smaller: it means less certain, not worse, and muting it would say the opposite.

  3. Recording coverage is a headline stat

    "7/7 nights with sleep data" sits beside the average, because an average over four nights and one over seven are not comparable and the user is the only person who can tell which they are looking at.

  4. One relationship, stated in words

    The card closes by naming the single thing the week's data actually shows. A screen of charts with no reading is a report, not an insight.

  1. Live hardware inverts; everything else does not

    The same inverted treatment as the Today strip, so a user who has seen one recognises the other. Inversion means this number is arriving right now, and it is used nowhere else in the app.

  2. A waveform, not a decorative pulse line

    The trace is the photoplethysmogram the node is actually transmitting. Drawing a generic heartbeat animation here would put a fictional graphic next to two real measurements on the same surface.

  3. Control sits beside data on one screen

    The lamp is actuation and the sensor is telemetry, but they are one physical device to the user. Splitting them into "sensors" and "controls" screens would describe the software's architecture rather than the object on their desk.

  4. Diagnostics are visible, not buried

    Signal strength, address, uptime and free memory are what actually explain a dropout on a prototype device. They stay on the face of the screen — this is a build to be demonstrated and debugged, and hiding them behind a developer toggle would cost more than the space they take.

  1. The hero states the payoff, not the product

    "Your recovery score is waiting — built from last night's sleep and your resting heart rate." A returning user is being told what is behind the door, and a first-time user learns what the app measures before being asked for anything.

  2. Focus state is drawn, not assumed

    Rest, focused and error are specified here because they are where a form actually fails. The focus ring is drawn as a separate outer layer — React Native has no shadow spread, so it has to be a real element rather than a style.

  3. The privacy line sits under the button

    Directly beneath the commit control, where the user is deciding whether to hand over health data — not in a policy screen they will never open.

  1. Why the ＋ carries three actions instead of a sixth tab

    The app has six destinations — Today, Insights, Movement, Nutrition, Device, Profile — and a tab bar tops out at five before the labels become unreadable. But the three that lost a tab share a property the other four do not: they are things the user does, not places they go. Grouping them behind one raised control turns an overflow problem into a statement about the app: four places to look, one button to record.

    Rejected

    A six-slot bar with icon-only tabs. Labels are what make a tab bar learnable, and dropping them to fit a sixth destination trades comprehension for capacity. Also rejected: a "More" tab, which hides the app's most frequent write actions two taps deep.

  2. AR lives here, and only here

    The camera-based movement session is a task with a beginning and an end, not a place to browse — so it is the first row of the action sheet rather than a tab. Its subtitle is recovery-aware, which makes the AR feature a consequence of the recovery score rather than a detached novelty.

  3. Rows are ordered by frequency, not by feature weight

    Meals, then sleep, then water. The AR session leads on visual weight because it is the most capable thing here, but the ordering below it follows how often a user will reach for each.

  4. The ＋ becomes the dismiss control

    It rotates 45° into an ✕ in place, so the control that opened the sheet is the control that closes it. No separate close affordance in a corner, and no reliance on a swipe the user has to discover.

Method

Screens were wireframed and argued in greyscale first. Only once the structure held were brand colour, gradient and depth applied, in a separate high-fidelity canvas.

States drawn elsewhere

Each component's states — daily brief pending / ready / failed, provisional versus established, node connected or absent, looked-up versus estimated, assistant offline — are specified on the component sheet in the hi-fi set and in docs/design/ui-spec.md.

Constraint noted throughout

Target is React Native, so no backdrop blur, no CSS grid, no shadow spread, and shadows behave differently on Android. Substitutions are recorded in the build spec rather than discovered during implementation.
